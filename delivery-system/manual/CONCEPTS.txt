CONCEPTS: WHAT THE WORDS MEAN
=============================
Part of the delivery manual, v0.3.

Read this once per workspace, and again whenever a term is unclear. The
last section says which part of the spec, proof-driven-delivery-system.txt,
explains the reasoning behind each idea. Read that section when you need
the why, not only the what.


People and ownership
--------------------

remit           An area of the product that one senior engineer answers
                for: a set of outcomes, with the flows, services and repos
                behind them. Remits follow outcomes, not repos, so one
                ticket can touch repos from several remits.
remit lead      The senior engineer who currently owns "what good looks
                like" for a remit. The role rotates. Approves the proofs
                for tiers 2 and 3.
remit page      The remit's standard, written in Atlas: criteria for good,
                exemplar contracts and bundles, verification skills, known
                traps, how many sessions are allowed, what may auto-merge.
outcome owner   The product person who owns a ticket's intent, answers
                challenges and accepts the result at UAT.
harness team    The engineers who own tracks, spotlights, fakers, the
                queue repo and this manual.


Knowledge
---------

Atlas           The team's knowledge repo, and the only source that binds
                you. It holds the North Star, principles, decisions, remit
                pages, agreed and legacy flows, the target state, lessons,
                minutes, and the weekly mark to market.
llm-wiki        The format Atlas is written in: a wiki kept for, and
                largely by, language-model sessions. It has an index page
                that you read first, pages that link to one another, an
                append-only log of what changed and when, and a schema page
                that says how the wiki is organised. If your organisation
                also keeps a separate wiki under that name, the config
                lists it under "knowledge". It is context only, and Atlas
                wins where they disagree.
North Star      The outcome everything is meant to move. Each ticket
                should link to a line of it, or to an objective under it.
mark to market  The weekly comparison of progress against that week's
                objectives. It tells you what is a priority now.
principle       A standing rule. Binding.
decision        A recorded choice. Binding; the newest wins, and a
                superseded one no longer counts.
agreed flow     How a flow is meant to behave today. Binding.
legacy flow     A flow being retired. Do not extend it.
target state    Where the system is heading. Binding, after agreed flows.
flow            One end-to-end behaviour of the product, such as "refund",
                usually across several services.
flow map        For each flow: how to reach it in a spotlight, how to
                drive it, its flags and data, known traps. Reference, not
                rule: check it against the product when it looks stale.
catalogs        Reference pages: services (what each owns and how to run
                it), fakers, spotlights, proofs (the regression suite by
                flow and rung), and the glossary.
project map     Which Jira project and component belongs to which remit,
                flows and repos. It is how you find the right context for
                a ticket.


Environments
------------

cluster         The group of services, from about twenty repos, that
                together make the product run.
test track      A full cluster in the cloud, with fakers standing in for
                systems the team does not own. There are few of them, so
                they are leased and reset to a baseline before each use.
spotlight       A named part of a cluster small enough to run inside a
                workspace. One fits at a time.
faker           A stand-in for a system the team does not own. It returns
                staged responses. Also called a digital twin.
faker drift     A faker that no longer behaves like the real system. The
                usual reason a change passes on a track and fails in UAT.
integrated UAT  The environment with real integrations in place of
                fakers. Product accepts work there.
lease           The right to use one test track for a limited time.
baseline track  A track that runs the full regression on the default
                branches continuously.
merge train     Several locally proven changes put on one track and proven
                together, to save track time.


Proof
-----

rung            One level of proof. Five of them make the proof ladder:
                  1 local     a spotlight in the workspace, with fakers
                  2 track     the full cluster, with fakers
                  3 UAT       real integrations; product accepts
                  4 prod      live, behind a flag; a signal is watched
                  5 regression  the proofs join the permanent suite
success         One thing that must be true when the work is done: what is
criterion       observed, the exact condition, and how it is observed.
proof scenario  A given, when, then case that shows criteria are met.
proof           An executable check for a scenario at a rung, with its
                saved output as evidence.
frozen proofs   The proof list once it is fixed, before implementation.
                From then on only the "passes" values may change.
holdout         A scenario product keeps where sessions cannot read it,
scenario        and runs at UAT. It stops an agent from fitting its work
                to the tests it can see. It is why you never claim "fully
                verified".
proof bundle    The evidence for an item: a summary, plus the saved
                outputs, transcripts and screenshots.
verification    A script with short instructions that drives one flow of
skill           the real product and returns pass or fail with evidence.
keep or revert  Each attempt ends at a commit no worse than the last, or
                is reset.
improvement     Work that is a number, not a feature, run as repeated
loop            measured experiments.


Work
----

item            One piece of work, named by its Jira key.
work contract   The item's definition of done: intent, scope, success
                criteria, proof scenarios, proofs by rung, tier, budget.
tier            How much autonomy a change gets, 0 to 3, set by what it
                touches. Higher tiers need more human approval.
budget          The hours, iterations and track runs an item may use
                before it must stop and ask.
stop condition  A situation in which you park the item and ask.
state           Where an item is: Draft, Ready, Claimed, Local-proven,
                Track-proven, UAT-proven, Live, Confirmed, Regressed. Or
                beside that line: Satisfied (already done), Cancelled,
                Decomposed (an epic split into stories).
hold            Why an item is waiting: parked, waiting-track,
                waiting-results, waiting-approval, waiting-answer,
                challenge.
challenge       A written objection to a ticket, for product to answer.


Coordination
------------

queue repo      The git repo through which sessions see each other.
claim           The branch claim/KEY in the queue repo. Its existence
                means someone holds the item.
card            The small file on a claim that says who holds it, which
                session is active, when it last saved, and what it
                touches.
session id      Engineer, machine and session: dana@ws-2#a41f.
active session  The one session allowed to work on an item right now.
heartbeat       The time of the active session's last save. Old means the
                session is probably gone.
board           One generated file listing every open item, question,
                track and request, so nobody reads hundreds of branches.
evidence        Where large evidence goes, so claims stay small.
branch
request         A note to the harness team that a capability is missing.
shared          environment.yaml: the facts that are the same for every
environment     workspace, published once.
level           What a workspace can do: 0 nothing yet, 1 local proof
                only, 2 plus tracks, 3 plus merge requests.
session cap     How many sessions an engineer in a remit may run. It
                starts at one and rises as the remit earns it.
